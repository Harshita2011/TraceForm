## Executive Summary
Customer acquisition grew 34% YoY, but net revenue retention dropped 500 bps to 91% as enterprise churn accelerated to 9%. The underlying cause is prolonged onboarding latency (averaging 48 days against a 21-day SLA) rather than competitive displacement. Immediate capital reallocation of $1.2M from top-of-funnel paid acquisition to customer engineering is required in Q4 to protect $14.5M in ARR renewals.

## Business Impact
- **Top-Line Variance:** Gross new ARR reached $8.4M (112% of target), yet churn eroded $3.8M, yielding net ARR expansion of only $4.6M.
- **Unit Economics:** Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) payback lengthened from 14 to 22 months due to first-year churn spikes.
- **Enterprise Cohort Contraction:** Accounts >$100k ARR accounted for 72% of churn volume, concentrated in the financial services vertical.

## Opportunities
1. **Onboarding Automation Lever:** Deploying standardized API integration connectors will reduce onboarding cycle time by 55%, capturing an estimated $2.8M in preserved first-year renewals.
2. **Expansion in Mid-Market:** Mid-market accounts (<$50k ARR) maintained 108% net retention with zero SLA breaches, representing an immediate low-CAC expansion path.

## Risks
- **Q4 Renewal Cliff:** $14.5M in enterprise ARR matures in Q4; without immediate engineering intervention on at-risk implementations, projected Q4 gross churn could reach 12%.
- **Team Burnout:** Solutions architect utilization sits at 94%, creating execution bottlenecks on Tier-1 deployments.
